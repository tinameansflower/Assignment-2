<summary><h3> Post Covid — Removing my mask  </h3></summary>
<h4> <em> When I blossomed </em> </h4>

<p> Click on the Sections for more Details</p>

  <details>
  <summary> <h6> <strong> My Contributions </strong> </h6> </summary>
  <p> Throughout COVID, I was incredibly anxious and self-conscious; I’d constantly wear a mask, even after it was no longer needed and there was no longer a need for it. I continued until 2024-2025. It was my safety net and helped me have enough courage to speak up. Though my anxiety continued to be terrible to the point where I’d tremble while presenting, even with moral support and sitting down while everyone looked at the screen. I had grown an attachment to it, but it also confined me, keeping me in my shell. I was incredibly insecure for 4 whole years until I finally ditched the mask. My senior year is when I really blossomed and met new people, let people get to know me, and found myself. That was also when I took off the mask.  
    </p>
    </details>

  <details>
  <summary> <h6> <strong> When did the Experience Occur </strong> </h6> </summary>
  
  <p> 2024 Feburary, my high school senior year.  </p>
    </details>


<details> 
      <summary> <h6> <strong> What I enjoyed </strong> </h6> </summary>
 
  <p> The good thing about a mask was that I could make silly faces, be in my safe zone, and just be a normal introvert, though that did cause me to keep to myself and talk to no one but the ones who interacted with me. Without the mask, I, in a way, learned who I was, who I wanted to be more clearly. I talked to people, felt like I was a part of a community, and really came out of my shell; it was nice and helped me learn tons of lessons. 

There are pros and cons with or without the mask, but I wouldn’t ever go back in time to change it; I learned and grew, learned lifelong lessons, along with my limits and what I am comfortable with. </p> 
</details>

<details> 
      <summary> <h6> <strong> Favourite Memory </strong> </h6> </summary>

 <p> I was able to meet various people, get close, and open up with people because they asked why I wore a mask when I look the way I do. My friends never cared; they never pressured me and let me open up at the pace I was safe with. I couldn’t be more grateful. As much as the mask confined me, it helped me find true friends. Funny enough, my favourite memory would be how, a while back, junior year, one of my close friends told me how she knew absolutely nothing about me and wanted to get to know me after being friends for 2 years and knowing each other for about 10. We’re still friends today, of course, but it's a funny memory because when I talked to her without my mask, she told me how I don’t feel like the Tina she knew and that I’m more out there and mature in a way. It’s silly, but something I remember. 

​ </p>
  </details>

<details> 
      <summary> <h6> <strong> What I didn't Enjoy </strong> </h6> </summary>
<p> Beginning without something covering my face after so long was incredibly nerve-racking. So many people knew me and had wondered what I actually looked like; my close friends already knew, but being as anxious and insecure as I was, I refused to go one step outside without it in fear of running into someone I knew or someone commenting on how I looked. A few loved ones as a kid always made comments on my appearance, so it was very overwhelming. That would be the only part, After taking it off though, I’d say it was very starting for people to actually come up to talk to me. Random people who I had no mutuals with. Otherwise, it wasn’t as bad as I imagined. 

​ </p>
