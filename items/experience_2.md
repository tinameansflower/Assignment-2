  <summary> <h6> <strong> My Contributions </strong> </h6> </summary>
  <p> In this job, I started out and took on various tasks, making drinks, serving food along with tea and water, cleaning washrooms, cups, and teacups, assembling the garnish for pho (bean sprouts, basil, cilantro),  clearing tables and cleaning them, and cashing customers out while maintaining good service and creating real bonds with the regulars and customers there. 
Originally, I applied to a Joy Sushi all-you-can-eat as a food runner; however, I got referred to this family-owned restaurant, which only has 3 employees total and needed help. Of course, I went to both interviews and ended up choosing Pho Viet King, mainly because of how kind the owners were, and the service and environment were incredibly welcoming and overall friendly; not to say Joy Sushi wasn’t- they were so kind to me, I just felt like I could connect more with customers and be more present.  
    </p>

<details> 
      <summary> <h6> <strong> What I enjoyed </strong> </h6> </summary>
 
  <p> The job offered lots of flexibility. As a student, flexibility was incredibly appreciated, and for the summer I planned to work full-time as much as I could to earn money to fund my education and lifestyle, rather than just rely on my parents and OSAP; this gave me a chance to be independent, live independently, and be a responsible and mature adult. Throughout my first year, I relied on my OSAP and saved money in whichever way I could; I disliked relying on my parents for money as I desired to be independent. Spending money was an option for me, not a requirement. Additionally, my first year was finally the chance I could be independent, and I loved it. This job was a path toward becoming who I wanted to be: hardworking, do-what-I-can, able to rely on myself, care for loved ones, and be a mature adult overall. Of course, that's the surface level, but I’ve created genuine bonds at my job with people I care for. If any takeaway were to come from the experience I’ve had is that you can truly turn any situation around; it's all in the matter of your approach and attitude, knowing that you are doing what u can and tht you cannot control those around you, only your own actions. That doesn’t mean to not try but rather to not dwell or stay stagnant over something out of your control. </p> 
</details>

 <p> My favourite memory would be not completely related to my job, but to be able to afford to treat my loved ones with gifts and things they deserve. <p> The trade-off of wanting to earn money and get as many shifts in as possible to fund my education was the lack of breaks that I had. I worked full-time the entire summer. My summer no longer became a summer; rather, the max I worked was about 97 hours in 2 weeks. It was brutal, considering there are only 4 employees total for an entire restaurant (including chefs). There is no designated job for us employees (except for chefs and the outside employees; 2 and 2); we only do what we can and help out others once finished. So for my first job outside of a family business, it was lots of adjustment and work, along with getting used to it, but I managed and persevered; so glad I did! </p>
